# Idea: Cave Diving (working title)

Requirements and ideas for prototype 2 of the Week 4 PCG assignment. This is a separate game from prototype 1 (Late, on the `pcg-prototype` branch). This is not a spec. A first playable version now exists, and the [README](README.md) describes what it does; the ideas below that it does not cover are still open.

## Overview

The player is a solo cave diver finding their way through a generated, flooded maze. No guideline is laid for them: they lay their own from a reel as they search for the end chamber, and have to get back out before their air runs out. The challenge is finding the way through, and deciding when to turn back, in a space that gets harder to see and harder to read.

## Requirements

- The core scenario is cave diving (洞穴潜水).
- The cave is generated by PCG, so each dive is different.
- The cave is a maze. The focus is finding the way through a generated maze, not a calm cave-diving simulation.
- No pre-laid guideline. The player lays their own line with the reel (tie in, lay, tie off).
- Level 0 is a tutorial that teaches laying a guideline before the real dives start.
- Built as a web page in HTML and JS.
- Anonymous, opt-in telemetry and a server-replayed leaderboard, the same way as Late and the Week 3 game, on this game's own Worker and D1 database. The game stays fully playable with both off or the Worker unreachable, and the generator and the movement and gas rules run without the browser so the Worker can replay a dive.
- The UI looks finished: no seams between tiles, and consistent spacing and alignment on every screen.
- The art concept comes first, made with ChatGPT image generation, including sprite sheets. Art and code are kept separate; generated art goes in `resources/`.
- The real accident stories on the Bilibili channel 神秘园 are a reference for how bad outcomes build up from small decisions. We borrow that decision structure only, and do not reuse any real incident, place or person.

## Ideas (all tentative)

**The world.** A maze of flooded passages: junctions, loops, dead ends, tight squeezes and larger chambers, with a solid ceiling (no air surface anywhere). Drawn in 2D as a side cross-section. Depth may be shown by color.

**The core loop.** Tie the reel in, find the way through the maze to the end chamber while laying line, and follow the line out. Air is the main resource, and the way back always costs air too. The key decisions are which way to go at each junction and when to turn back. The rule of thirds is the model for the turn.

**What gets generated.** The maze layout (graph first, then the shape of the passages); the squeezes and chambers; where the goal is; how much line is on the reel; and the starting air and conditions.

**Silt.** Kicking up silt in tight spots lowers visibility, and this also affects the way back out. Because the player's own earlier moves make the return harder, the order of moves matters, not only the route.

**The guideline.** Laid by the player, and the only reliable piece of information when visibility drops. Possible extras: markers the player places on their line, and a limited number of tie-offs.

**Less information over time.** A full map at first; later the map shown only at the entrance and then hidden; finally only the line and the lamp. This is a display setting, not generated content. The map shows the passages, never the way through.

**Failure feedback.** After a failed dive, a short generated timeline built from the run log (when silt was stirred up, when the turn-back point was passed, and so on). Text templates are hand-made, and the data comes from the run.

**Teammates.** A possible extension: a teammate who only knows what the player told them.

**Adaptivity.** A Director-style layer could ease or tighten conditions between dives from how the player has been doing. It adjusts settings and does not generate space. Using Jev for this is optional.

**Seeds.** The same seed gives the same cave, and the seed is shown so a dive can be replayed.

**Telemetry and leaderboard.** A dive is fully determined by its seed and its inputs, so the server can replay it. What telemetry records should feed the level dials: where divers turn, how often they silt out, and whether they lay a line. Beyond one daily board, there could be weekly or per-level boards.

**Hand-made.** Rules, art, sound, and text templates.

**Tech.** A static page with no build step, drawn on a canvas. rot.js may help with seeded randomness and cave shapes.

## Art

- Art concept first, using ChatGPT image generation, then sprite sheets. These now exist in `resources/`:
  - `cave_concept_1.png`: side-view scene (diver, guideline, lamp cone, silt).
  - `cave_concept_2.png`: top-down survey-style map (depth bands, guideline with markers, dashed jump, faint unexplored outlines).
  - `cave_sprite.png`: diver sprite sheet.
  - `cave_sprite2.png`: tiles, props, guideline pieces, silt and light effects, bubbles.
  - `sprites/`: frames and props cut from the two sheets by `tools/cut_sprites.py`, for the game to use.
- Direction: deep teal, ink blue and slate grey, with warm amber reserved for the guideline, the reel and the headlamp; a narrow light cone, drifting silt, no visible surface above.
- The generated sprite grids are not pixel-aligned (frame spacing is uneven), so frames are cut and aligned by script. The tiles do not join seamlessly on their own, so the game uses one rock tile blended to repeat.

## Checking generated dives

Each generated maze should be checked to be winnable within the air budget, with room for wrong turns and enough line on the reel, and regenerated if not.

## Open questions

- How long a dive should last in real time, and whether a session is one dive or a series.
- Whether the difficulty measures we can compute match how hard a dive feels to players.
- Whether the leaderboard is worth the Cloudflare Workers Paid plan it needs to replay dives.
- Whether Jev can be called from a static page.
- The cave diving terms and rules used above were checked against search excerpts of the sources listed in the README, not the full pages.
